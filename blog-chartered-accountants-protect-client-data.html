Accountants and tax preparers: how to protect client tax and ID files on your laptop

An accountant's laptop is a vault of other people's lives: tax returns, payslips and wage statements, bank statements, ID copies, invoices and balance sheets. Clients trust you to keep them confidential, your professional code expects it, and data-protection laws in many countries expect reasonable security for personal data. Here's how to protect client files on an ordinary office PC or laptop, without changing how you work.

Short answer: keep client documents encrypted on your PC, not in plain folders. A file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt seals each file with AES-256-GCM, works offline, and needs no client data to leave your computer. Use a separate encrypted vault per client group, let a watch folder encrypt new downloads automatically, and keep an encrypted backup. Encryption is one of the security measures named in data-protection laws such as the EU's GDPR (Article 32).

Why this matters for an accounting or tax practice

  • Professional duty: confidentiality is one of the five fundamental principles of the IESBA International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants, which is used in more than 130 jurisdictions. Information you get from clients must be kept confidential.
  • Data-protection law: laws in many places expect reasonable security for personal data. The EU's GDPR (Article 32) lists "the pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data" among appropriate security measures. California's CCPA lets consumers sue when their nonencrypted and nonredacted personal information is stolen because a covered business didn't maintain reasonable security. India's DPDP Rules, 2025 (Rule 6) also name encryption among the security safeguards.
  • In the US: the FTC Safeguards Rule covers tax preparation firms and requires them to encrypt customer information on their systems and in transit (or use approved alternative controls). The IRS points tax professionals to Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data, and to a written information security plan.
  • Everyday risks: a stolen laptop, a staff member's USB stick, a shared office PC, a repair shop, or a phishing attack.

Set it up in 15 minutes

  1. Download Clavis Encrypt and install it (Windows 10 or 11; no administrator rights or internet needed).
  2. Create a vault with a strong password, and store the one-time recovery key somewhere safe, for example printed and kept in the office safe.
  3. Encrypt existing client folders. With Clavis Pro, right-click a folder → Clavis → Encrypt to do the whole folder at once. Clavis Free encrypts one file or many at once, so you can also select a folder's files and encrypt them together. Client ID copies, payslips and bank statements that arrived by email or a messaging app are easy to forget; Clavis Pro's Find sensitive files helps you find ID, bank, salary and tax documents on your PC and encrypt them together (where forgotten ID and bank scans hide).
  4. Make a dedicated folder for client downloads (returns and notices from tax-authority portals, bank exports, payroll reports), save downloads there, and add it as a watch folder (Pro). Every new file is encrypted automatically once it finishes saving.
  5. Open a file when you need it: double-click the .enc file. With Pro's Open, you edit it in Excel or Word and it's re-locked when you're done; the temporary working copy is cleaned up.
  6. Use separate vaults (Pro) for different partners or client groups, each with its own password.

Good practice for the whole office

  • Never email unprotected ID copies or tax documents. Clavis's free Send with a password makes a locked copy your client opens in their browser, with the password sent by text or call. See how to encrypt files before emailing.
  • Collect only what you need, and ask for copies with ID numbers partly hidden where the full number isn't needed.
  • Encrypt before the cloud. If you keep client files in Google Drive or OneDrive, store only encrypted copies there (Clavis Premium can encrypt files straight into your cloud folder). See encrypting before the cloud.
  • Keep backups encrypted too, and delete files you no longer need to keep. Clavis's free Shred overwrites them before deleting (best effort on SSDs and flash).
  • Turn on full-disk encryption (BitLocker or Device encryption) as a second layer for lost laptops.

Questions

How should an accountant store client tax documents on a laptop?

Keep them encrypted on your PC rather than in ordinary folders, never send unprotected copies by email or messaging apps, keep encrypted backups, and delete copies you no longer need. A file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt seals each file with AES-256-GCM and works offline, and full-disk encryption adds a second layer for a lost laptop.

Does GDPR require encryption?

Not in every case. Article 32 of the GDPR requires security appropriate to the risk and lists "the pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data" as an example of appropriate measures. For client files on laptops and PCs, encryption is usually the most practical of them.

Do tax preparers in the US have to encrypt client data?

The FTC Safeguards Rule covers tax preparation firms and requires them to encrypt customer information on their systems and in transit, unless a qualified person approves effective alternative controls. The IRS's Publication 4557, Safeguarding Taxpayer Data, explains these responsibilities, including a written information security plan.

How do I send a tax return to a client securely?

Encrypt it before sending. Clavis Encrypt's free Send with a password makes a locked copy the client opens in their browser; send the password separately by text or call, never in the same email.

Will encrypted files still open in Excel, Word or a PDF reader?

Yes. Open the encrypted file in Clavis and it opens in its usual program. With Clavis Pro's Open, your changes are saved back into the encrypted file when you're done and the temporary working copy is cleaned up.

Do client files leave my computer with Clavis Encrypt?

No. Encryption happens on your PC. There's no account or cloud service, and the app goes online only to download signed updates.
