Clavis vs Cryptomator vs AxCrypt vs BitLocker: which file encryption should you use?

"Encryption" covers very different jobs. BitLocker protects a lost laptop; Cryptomator protects a vault folder in your cloud; AxCrypt and Clavis protect individual files. Here's an honest comparison, starting with the question people ask most (AxCrypt or Cryptomator?), and including where Clavis is not the best choice.

Short answer: choose Cryptomator if you want a free, open-source encrypted vault inside Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive and you work inside that vault every day; it needs no account. Choose AxCrypt if you want to encrypt individual files on Windows, macOS and mobile and don't mind an AxCrypt account and a subscription (its free plan is for testing). Turn on BitLocker or Windows device encryption on every laptop, whatever else you use. Clavis Encrypt is for encrypting individual files on Windows or Linux with no account and no subscription; it's free for everyday use, but it's closed source and has no macOS or mobile app.

BitLocker: turn it on, but it's not file encryption

If your Windows edition has BitLocker or device encryption, use it. It's the right protection for a lost or stolen laptop. But once Windows is running, every file is readable as normal — by you, by malware, by anyone at your desk — and a file you copy to a USB stick or email is not encrypted at all. Not sure which one you have? See device encryption vs BitLocker on Windows 11 Home.

Cryptomator: great for a cloud vault

Cryptomator is open source and well regarded. It keeps an encrypted vault inside your cloud folder and shows it as a virtual drive while unlocked. Choose it if you want to work on files inside a vault all day and you want open source. It's less suited to encrypting a file here and there, or sending one to someone.

AxCrypt: file encryption with an account

AxCrypt encrypts individual files and integrates with Explorer. You sign in with an AxCrypt account, and its free plan is described by AxCrypt as limited use for testing; unlimited encryption is part of its Premium and Business subscriptions.

Clavis: file encryption without an account or subscription

Clavis Encrypt encrypts files in place with AES-256-GCM and Argon2id, gives encrypted files random names, and works from the Explorer right-click menu. No account, nothing uploaded. Free encrypts one file or several at once, unlocks with Windows Hello, sends a file with a one-time password that opens in any browser, and shreds files you no longer need (best effort on SSDs and flash). Pro ($19 once) adds whole folders, watch folders, finding forgotten ID and bank documents on your PC, opening files without a decrypted copy, secure notes, key-file 2FA and multiple vaults. Premium ($29 once) adds encrypting into cloud folders, sharing with people without a shared password, backups with previous versions, and a command line. Pro and Premium are one-time purchases; everything in Free stays free.

Where Clavis isn't the best fit: the app is closed source and hasn't been independently audited yet (its file format is public, with an open-source decryptor, so your files never depend on it); there's no macOS or mobile app, though .enc files open in any browser; and it isn't full-disk encryption — use BitLocker (or LUKS on Linux) for that, alongside it.

Which should you use?

  • Laptop protection: BitLocker / device encryption. Everyone should have it on.
  • An open-source vault in your cloud: Cryptomator.
  • Encrypting files on Windows, macOS and phones, and sharing with other AxCrypt users: AxCrypt, if an account and a subscription suit you.
  • Encrypting, sharing and backing up individual files on Windows or Linux, without an account: Clavis.

Questions

Cryptomator vs AxCrypt: which is better?

They do different jobs. Cryptomator is free and open source on desktop, needs no account, and keeps an encrypted vault folder inside your cloud storage. AxCrypt encrypts individual files, needs an AxCrypt account, and its unlimited use is a paid subscription. Pick Cryptomator for a cloud vault, AxCrypt for account-based file encryption and sharing with other AxCrypt users.

Is Cryptomator free?

On Windows, macOS and Linux, yes: Cryptomator says all functional and encryption features are free on the desktop. Its mobile apps are free to read vaults, and writing needs a one-time purchase. Cryptomator Hub for teams has paid plans.

Does AxCrypt need an account?

Yes. AxCrypt's own Windows guide says to sign in with your AxCrypt account, and new users create an account and verify their email before encrypting files.

Is AxCrypt open source?

Partly. AxCrypt says important parts of its code are available for inspection in a public GitHub repository for its .NET desktop app. Cryptomator describes itself as open source.

Is BitLocker enough, or do I need file encryption too?

BitLocker or device encryption protects the drive when the PC is off or stolen. It doesn't protect files while you're signed in, or copies on a USB stick, in email or in the cloud. For those, add file encryption such as Clavis, AxCrypt or a Cryptomator vault.

What's a free alternative to AxCrypt with no account?

Cryptomator (a vault for cloud folders) and Clavis Encrypt (individual files on Windows and Linux) both work without an account. Clavis Free encrypts one or several files at once, with a recovery key; whole folders are part of Clavis Pro.
