How to keep your private photos and videos private on your laptop

Your laptop gets passed around. A friend borrows it for a group project, you share your screen on a call, a sibling uses it for an exam form, and Google Photos or OneDrive quietly syncs your camera roll. Personal photos and videos end up in folders anyone can open. Here's how to keep them private without making the laptop hard to use.

Short answer: move private photos and videos into encrypted files instead of hiding them in a folder. With a free app such as Clavis Encrypt, each file is sealed with AES-256-GCM and gets a random name. Friends using your laptop, a screen-share, a repair shop, or someone copying your drive see only locked files they can't open or even identify. Turn off automatic photo backup for anything you want kept off the cloud.

Where private photos usually leak from

  • Friends or family using your laptop: Photos, Downloads and WhatsApp Desktop folders are one click away.
  • Screen sharing: a Photos app or a folder of thumbnails pops up while you're presenting on Meet, Zoom or Teams.
  • Automatic sync: phone backups to Google Photos, OneDrive or iCloud copy everything, including pictures you meant to keep only on one device.
  • Repairs and resale: repair shops often ask for your password, and deleted photos can be recovered from a sold laptop.
  • Hidden folders: Windows' "hidden" setting is easy to turn off, and searches still find the files.

Lock them in 5 minutes

  1. Install Clavis Encrypt (free; Windows 10, Windows 11 and Linux). There's no account, and nothing is uploaded.
  2. Create a vault with a password only you know. Don't reuse your Instagram or Windows password. Save the one-time recovery key somewhere private.
  3. Select the photos and videos you want private, right-click → Clavis → Encrypt. Each becomes a .enc file with a random name, and no thumbnail preview.
  4. To look at one, double-click it and enter your password. Close it and it's locked again.
  5. With Clavis Pro, encrypt a whole folder at once, or make a watch folder that encrypts anything you save into it automatically.
  6. Check your phone's backup settings, and exclude private albums from Google Photos or OneDrive backup if you don't want them in the cloud.

Good habits

  • Before lending your laptop, lock Clavis (it also locks itself when Windows locks or the laptop sleeps), or give the friend a separate Windows guest account.
  • Before screen sharing, share a single window rather than your whole screen.
  • Before selling or repairing the laptop, make sure private files are encrypted, and shred copies you no longer want with Clavis's free Shred (best effort on SSDs and flash). See protecting files before a laptop repair.
  • Sharing with one person? Encrypt the file for them with their Clavis ID instead of sending it openly. See sharing encrypted files.

If someone threatens to leak your photos

Don't pay, and don't delete the messages. Take screenshots, block the account, and report it to the police or your country's cybercrime reporting service, for example the FBI's IC3 in the US or cybercrime.gov.in (helpline 1930) in India. If you're an adult, StopNCII.org helps stop intimate images from being shared on major platforms; if the images were taken when you were under 18, use Take It Down. It isn't your fault, and help is available.

Questions

How do I hide private photos on my laptop from friends and family?

Don't just hide them; encrypt them. A free app such as Clavis Encrypt turns each photo into a locked file with a random name and no thumbnail, which can only be opened with your password. Lock the app before you lend your laptop.

Can I password-protect a folder of photos on Windows 11?

Windows has no simple folder password. Use a file-encryption app: Clavis Encrypt encrypts photos one by one for free, and Clavis Pro encrypts a whole folder at once or automatically.

Is a hidden folder safe for private photos?

No. Anyone can turn on "show hidden files", and Windows search still finds them. Hidden files are also fully readable if the laptop is repaired, sold or copied.

Will encrypted photos still upload to Google Photos or OneDrive?

If a synced folder contains encrypted files, only the encrypted versions are uploaded, which no one can view without your password. Your phone's own backup is separate, so check its settings too.

What should I do if someone is blackmailing me with my photos?

Don't pay and don't delete the evidence. Screenshot the messages, block the account, and report it to the police or your country's cybercrime reporting service (such as IC3 in the US). Adults can use StopNCII.org to help stop the images spreading; for images taken under 18, use Take It Down.
