Is it safe to keep ID scans (passport, ID card, tax ID) on your laptop? How to protect them

Most people have a folder like this: passport, national ID card front and back, driving licence, a tax ID card, a voided cheque, maybe a bank statement, saved for the next time a bank, landlord or employer asks for proof of identity. It's convenient. It's also a ready-made identity kit for anyone who gets hold of your laptop, phone or email. Here's how to keep those scans and still have them handy.

Short answer: yes, if they're encrypted. Plain scans in a "Documents" or "ID" folder can be read by anyone who gets your laptop: a thief, a repair shop, or someone who borrows it. Encrypt the files with a free tool such as Clavis Encrypt (AES-256-GCM, works offline), share a copy with most of the number covered wherever the full number isn't needed, and don't leave ID copies in your email, WhatsApp or Downloads folder.

The real risks of unprotected ID scans

  • Lost or stolen laptops and phones: a Windows or phone lock screen doesn't encrypt a file someone can copy off the drive.
  • Repair shops often ask for your password and have full access while the device is with them. See protecting your files before a laptop repair.
  • Shared family PCs: everyone with access can open the folder. See keeping files private on a shared PC.
  • Old copies everywhere: email attachments, WhatsApp media, Downloads and cloud backups quietly keep copies for years.
  • Misuse: ID copies can be used for fraudulent loan applications, phone contracts or accounts opened in your name.

How to protect your ID documents (step by step)

  1. Collect them in one folder. Search your PC, Downloads, email and WhatsApp Desktop for "passport", "id card", "licence", "statement" and "tax", and move or delete the stray copies. Clavis can help you find the ones you've forgotten: see below.
  2. Install Clavis Encrypt (free; Windows 10, Windows 11 and Linux), create a vault with a strong password, and keep the one-time recovery key safe.
  3. Encrypt each scan: right-click → Clavis → Encrypt. The file becomes a .enc file with a random name, so even the name "passport" isn't visible.
  4. When a form asks for a copy, double-click the encrypted file to open it, upload it, and close it. It stays encrypted on your disk.
  5. Share less where you can. If an organisation doesn't need your full ID number, ask whether a copy with most of the number covered is acceptable, and consider writing what the copy is for ("For the rental application only") across it.
  6. Before sending a copy by email, encrypt it. In Clavis, right-click the scan → Send with a password… (free): the other side opens it in their browser, and you send the password separately. See how to encrypt files before emailing, or password-protect the PDF.

Find the scans you've forgotten

The hard part is often remembering where the copies are: an ID card photo saved as IMG_2041.jpg, a bank statement from three years ago in Downloads, a passport scan someone sent you on WhatsApp. Find sensitive files in Clavis Encrypt Pro helps you find them, so you can encrypt them in one go (encrypting files one at a time is free). For a full walk-through of where these copies hide on a Windows PC, including the hidden WhatsApp Desktop folder and saved email attachments, see how to find forgotten ID, bank and tax document scans on your PC.

  1. Open Clavis and choose Find Sensitive Files in the sidebar.
  2. Under Where to look, keep Documents, Downloads, Desktop and Pictures and, if you use WhatsApp Desktop, the files received in WhatsApp. Add a folder… covers anything else, such as an old "Scans" folder.
  3. Leave Read text inside pictures and PDFs on. Clavis then uses the text recognition built into Windows to read photos and scanned PDFs, so a photographed bank statement or payslip can be spotted whatever the file is called.
  4. Press Start search. Files appear as Likely sensitive (such as bank statements, salary slips and files named "passport") or Possibly sensitive (such as medical reports, cheques and files named "income tax"), each with the reason it's listed.
  5. Untick anything that isn't sensitive and press Encrypt selected.
  6. For extra copies you don't need at all, right-click → Clavis → Shred… (or use the Shred Files page) overwrites and deletes them. It's best effort on SSDs, USB sticks and memory cards, and backups or cloud version history may still keep a copy, so delete those too.

Bank statements and payslips are recognised by their content. For ID cards, passports and tax forms, Clavis mostly goes by the file name: it reads the ID number itself only for a few documents today (India's Aadhaar, PAN, passport and voter ID cards and its income-tax forms), so look through your folders for the rest. The search runs only on your PC: nothing is uploaded, and Clavis doesn't keep a list of your files. It can miss some documents, such as blurry photos or files with unusual names, so "No sensitive files found" means nothing matched in the folders you chose, not that there's nothing to protect. On Linux it checks file names and documents, but not the text in pictures and PDFs. Step-by-step help: Find sensitive files on your PC.

For scans and photocopies you keep on your own computer, file encryption is what protects them. Full-disk encryption (BitLocker or Device encryption) is a good second layer for a lost laptop, but it doesn't protect copies you email, sync or put on a USB stick.

If you're in India: a masked Aadhaar shows only the last four digits of the number and can be downloaded from myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in. In May 2022 UIDAI advised sharing masked copies instead of photocopies, then withdrew that advisory two days later because it could be misinterpreted; the official position is to use "normal prudence" when sharing Aadhaar. A masked copy is still the safer choice wherever the full number isn't needed, and DigiLocker is a good home for documents issued digitally.

Questions

Is it safe to keep a scan of my passport or ID card on my laptop?

It's safe if the file is encrypted. A plain scan can be read by anyone who gets the laptop, including thieves and repair shops. Encrypt it with a file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt, and share a copy with most of the number covered where the full number isn't needed.

Should I send a copy of my ID on WhatsApp or by email?

Avoid it where you can. Copies stay in chats, email and backups for years and are easy to forward. Share only what's needed, and encrypt or password-protect files you send (Clavis's free Send with a password makes a locked copy that opens in any browser).

How do I find old ID and bank document scans on my laptop?

Search File Explorer for "passport", "id card", "statement" and "tax", and check Downloads, Desktop and WhatsApp Desktop. Find sensitive files in Clavis Encrypt Pro helps: on your PC, it reads file names, the text in documents and, on Windows, the text in pictures and PDFs, then lets you tick which files to encrypt. It recognises bank statements and payslips by content but most ID cards only by file name, and it can miss blurry photos or files with unusual names, so look through your usual folders too.

How do I password-protect a scan of my ID card?

Encrypt the file with Clavis Encrypt (right-click → Clavis → Encrypt), or use a PDF tool that adds a password. Encryption hides the file's name too, so it isn't obvious what the file is.
