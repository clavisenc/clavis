Device encryption vs BitLocker: does Windows 11 Home have either?

Search for “BitLocker Windows 11 Home” and you meet two names for what sounds like one thing. Here's how they differ, how to tell whether your PC is already encrypted, what the Device Encryption Support line in System Information means, and what neither one protects.

Short answer: Windows 11 Home doesn't have the full BitLocker tool, but many Home PCs have Device encryption, which is BitLocker encryption switched on automatically with fewer controls. It needs a TPM and Secure Boot, and it only protects the drive after an administrator signs in with a Microsoft account, or you sign in with a work or school account.

Device encryption vs BitLocker, side by side

They use the same encryption. Microsoft calls device encryption “a Windows feature that provides a simple way for some devices to enable BitLocker encryption automatically”. The differences are in who turns it on and how much you control.

The account row matters most. Automatic device encryption starts during Windows setup, but Microsoft says protection only switches on after an administrator signs in with a Microsoft account, or you sign in with a work or school account; that's also when the recovery key is backed up. With only local accounts, Windows either doesn't turn device encryption on at all or leaves the drive encrypted but unprotected (Microsoft's pages describe both). Either way, the drive isn't protected until an administrator signs in with a Microsoft account.

Is my PC already encrypted? How to check for Device encryption

Signed in as an administrator, open Start → Settings → Privacy & security → Device encryption: if the switch says On, device encryption is on. For the exact state of a drive, run manage-bde -status C: in Terminal (Admin): Conversion Status says whether it's encrypted, and Protection Status says whether that encryption is protecting it. Device Encryption Support: what System Information is telling you

Before you change any BIOS/UEFI setting (TPM, Secure Boot or a firmware update), check whether any drive is encrypted (see above). If one is, find its recovery key first (see below), then suspend BitLocker for the change: how to suspend it, including what to do on Windows Home. Otherwise the PC can ask for the recovery key at the next restart.
  1. Open Start and type System Information.
  2. Right-click it and choose Run as administrator.
  3. Under System Summary, find Device Encryption Support (on some PCs, Automatic Device Encryption Support).

Microsoft's hardware list is a TPM (1.2 or 2.0), UEFI Secure Boot and Platform Secure Boot. Before Windows 11 version 24H2, a PC also needed Modern Standby or HSTI support and no un-allowed DMA ports. Those two requirements were removed in 24H2, so more PCs qualify now. If your PC didn't qualify and you fix the cause, for example by turning on Secure Boot, device encryption turns on by itself once Windows detects the change, unless it was turned off before (then use the switch in Settings). It still only protects the drive once an administrator has signed in with a Microsoft account, so check aka.ms/myrecoverykey afterwards. OSV vs FDV: what those drive labels mean

You'll most often see these letters in the Drive column of the recovery keys page in your Microsoft account (aka.ms/myrecoverykey), and in IT forums. The V stands for volume. BitLocker policy names use similar short forms, such as OSEncryptionType, FDVEncryptionType and RDVEncryptionType. To pick the right recovery key, match the key ID rather than the label.

LabelWhat it isDevice encryptionBitLocker

Find your recovery key before you need it

With BitLocker or device encryption, a firmware update, a hardware change or a repair can make Windows ask for the recovery key at startup: a 48-digit number. Without it, the drive stays locked, with all your files. For device encryption, the key is in the Microsoft account of the administrator who first signed in to the PC (often you, but not always): open aka.ms/myrecoverykey on another device and match the key ID shown on the recovery screen. For a work or school account, use aka.ms/aadrecoverykey or ask your IT team. If someone else set up your PC, the key may be in their Microsoft account. Look it up now and keep a copy somewhere other than the laptop, especially before you hand it over for repair. Keep it private: anyone with the key can unlock the drive. Full steps, including what to do if the key isn't in your account: how to find your BitLocker recovery key.

To check that the key in your account is the one for this drive today (an account can hold old keys from an earlier install or another PC), run manage-bde -protectors -get C: in Terminal (Admin) and compare the ID under Numerical Password with the key ID at aka.ms/myrecoverykey. (On Windows Home, Microsoft doesn't say whether manage-bde works; if it gives an error, make sure your account lists a key for this PC.) If no key matches, back it up now from Manage BitLocker on Pro, or by signing in with an administrator's Microsoft account on Home, before any firmware update or repair. Without the key, Microsoft's only way back in is a reset that removes your files.

Should you upgrade to Windows 11 Pro for BitLocker?

Only if you need what device encryption lacks: a PIN before Windows starts, control over each drive, a recovery key kept off Microsoft's servers, or encrypted USB sticks with BitLocker To Go. For the most common worry, a stolen laptop, device encryption already does the job if your PC supports it and an administrator has signed in with a Microsoft account.

What drive encryption doesn't do

Device encryption and BitLocker protect the drive while the PC is off. Once Windows has started and you're signed in, every file is readable to anyone using your account: a family member, the repair shop you gave your password to, or malware. And the moment you copy a file somewhere else, it isn't encrypted any more: a USB stick, an email attachment or the copy OneDrive or Google Drive uploads to the cloud.

That's where file-level encryption fits, as a second layer, not a replacement. Options include an encrypted archive in 7-Zip, a VeraCrypt container (protected while it's closed), or a file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt, which encrypts individual files with AES-256-GCM so they stay locked while the PC is on, inside cloud folders and on USB sticks. Clavis is free for individual files and runs on Windows 11 Home without administrator rights; encrypting a whole folder in one step is a paid feature. It's closed source and hasn't been independently audited, though its file format is public.

Questions

Is device encryption the same as BitLocker?

Nearly. Device encryption uses the same BitLocker encryption, but it isn't the full BitLocker tool. It turns BitLocker on automatically for the PC's internal drives on supported hardware, unlocks with the TPM chip and saves the recovery key to your Microsoft, work or school account. Full BitLocker, in Windows 11 Pro, Enterprise and Education, lets you choose drives, add a startup PIN, decide where the recovery key goes and encrypt USB sticks.

Does Windows 11 Home have BitLocker?

Not the BitLocker tool. Microsoft says BitLocker Drive Encryption isn't available on Windows Home edition. Many Windows 11 Home PCs have device encryption instead, which is BitLocker turned on automatically with fewer options. Check Settings, Privacy & security, Device encryption.

What is the difference between device encryption and BitLocker in Windows 11?

Device encryption is the automatic, simplified version: one switch in Settings, internal drives only, recovery key in your Microsoft, work or school account, and it works on Windows 11 Home. BitLocker is the manual, full version in Pro, Enterprise and Education, with per-drive control, startup PINs, more recovery-key options and BitLocker To Go for removable drives.

How do I know if my PC is already encrypted?

Check Settings, Privacy & security, Device encryption, or run manage-bde -status C: in Terminal (Admin). How to check if your drive is encrypted explains each result.

What does Device Encryption Support mean in System Information?

It tells you whether your PC can use device encryption: Meets prerequisites means it can, and other values name the reason it can't, such as TPM is not usable. How to fix each one: device encryption not available in Windows 11.

What is automatic device encryption?

It's device encryption switching itself on. After Windows setup on a PC that meets the requirements, Windows starts encrypting the internal drives and finishes protecting them when an administrator signs in with a Microsoft account, or you sign in with a work or school account, which backs up the recovery key. With only local accounts, the drive isn't protected: Windows either doesn't turn it on or leaves it encrypted but unprotected.

What is the difference between OSV and FDV drives?

OSV is the operating system volume, the drive Windows runs from. FDV is a fixed data volume: another internal drive or partition. RDV is a removable data volume, such as a USB stick. You see these labels on your Microsoft account's recovery keys page. Device encryption covers OSV and FDV drives, not RDV drives.

Do I need device encryption on Windows 11?

If your PC supports it, keep it on: Microsoft recommends it, and it's what protects a lost or stolen laptop. Just make sure you can find the recovery key first. Thinking of turning it off? See turning off device encryption in Windows 11.

Is device encryption as secure as BitLocker?

The encryption itself is the same. Device encryption uses XTS-AES 128-bit by default and unlocks automatically with the TPM. BitLocker adds options such as a startup PIN, which also protects against someone who has the PC but not the PIN. For a typical stolen laptop, both keep the drive unreadable.

Does device encryption encrypt my USB drive?

No. Microsoft says device encryption covers the Windows drive and fixed internal drives, not external or USB drives. To encrypt the whole stick, use BitLocker To Go (Pro, Enterprise or Education) or VeraCrypt. To encrypt chosen files on it, use a 7-Zip archive or a file-encryption app such as Clavis Encrypt, which work on Home.

Why don't I have device encryption on my PC?

Your PC may not meet the requirements, such as a TPM and Secure Boot, or you may be signed in with a standard account. Device encryption not available in Windows 11 shows how to find the reason and fix it.

Where is my device encryption recovery key?

In the Microsoft account of the administrator who first signed in to the PC: open aka.ms/myrecoverykey on another device and pick the key whose ID matches the recovery screen. Work or school PCs, and what to do if it isn't there: how to find your BitLocker recovery key.

If device encryption is on, do I still need file encryption?

For your most private files, it helps. Device encryption protects the drive when the PC is off. It doesn't protect files while you're signed in, or copies on a USB stick, in email or in OneDrive or Google Drive. File-level encryption, with a tool such as 7-Zip or Clavis Encrypt (free for individual files; closed source and not independently audited), keeps chosen files encrypted in those places. A VeraCrypt container does the same while it's closed.
