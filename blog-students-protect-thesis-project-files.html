Students: how to protect your thesis, project and exam files from being copied

Months of work on a thesis, a dissertation, a capstone project or research data, sitting on a laptop your roommates borrow, a lab PC, or a USB stick that goes from hand to hand. Students lose work to copying, accidental deletion and stolen laptops every term. Here's how to keep yours private and safe.

Short answer: keep your thesis, code and data encrypted on your laptop and USB stick, and keep at least two backups. A free app such as Clavis Encrypt locks each file with AES-256-GCM and a random file name, so a classmate using your laptop or a lost USB stick exposes nothing. When you share with your group or supervisor, share a copy, not your only one.

Common ways student work gets copied or lost

  • Shared laptops in dorms, shared flats and labs, where anyone can open your Documents folder.
  • USB sticks passed around for printing or submissions, and lost.
  • Group projects where one copy becomes everyone's copy.
  • No backup: one crash, theft or spilled coffee a week before the deadline.

Protect your work (10 minutes)

  1. Install Clavis Encrypt (free; Windows 10, 11 and Linux).
  2. Create a vault with a password your friends don't know, and save the one-time recovery key.
  3. Encrypt the files you want private: thesis drafts, datasets, code archives, scanned certificates and transcripts.
  4. To work on your thesis, open it, edit and save. Pro's Open re-locks it when you close Word.
  5. Copy encrypted files to your USB stick. They stay encrypted there, so a lost stick exposes nothing. See encrypting a USB drive. To open them on a lab or library PC without installing anything, see carrying encrypted files on a USB stick.
  6. Back up twice: once to a cloud folder (encrypted copies are fine there) and once to a USB stick or external disk.

Prove the work is yours

  • Keep dated drafts and versions. Clavis Premium keeps up to 10 previous versions of files you edit with Open.
  • Email yourself, or your supervisor, dated drafts at milestones.
  • Keep your raw data and notes. They're the strongest evidence of original work.

Questions

How do I stop someone copying my project from my laptop?

Encrypt the files. With a free app such as Clavis Encrypt, your thesis and project files can only be opened with your password and have random names, so someone using your laptop can't open or even identify them.

How do I password-protect a USB flash drive for school or university?

Copy encrypted files onto it. Files encrypted with Clavis Encrypt stay encrypted on the stick and open on any PC with Clavis and your password. Windows Home has no BitLocker To Go, so file encryption is the simplest option there.

What's the best way to back up a thesis or dissertation?

Keep at least two copies besides your laptop, for example one cloud folder and one USB stick or external disk, and update them weekly. Encrypted copies are safe to keep in the cloud.

Is Clavis Encrypt free for students?

Yes. Encrypting and opening individual files is free, with full-strength encryption. Paid editions add folders, automation and version history.
