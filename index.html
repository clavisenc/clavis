01Your laptop goes places

Your Windows sign-in doesn't lock your files

It protects the screen, not the files themselves. Four everyday ways that matters, and what fixes each one.

At the repair shop

The shop may ask for your Windows password. With it, they can open every file in Documents.

Encrypt those files before you hand it over, and turn off Unlock with Windows Hello if they'll have your PIN. They stay locked while the laptop is away. Protect files before a repair →
On a shared family PC

Anyone who signs in as you, or uses the PC while you're signed in, can open your payslips, ID scans and statements.

Lock them with a password only you know, separate from your Windows sign-in. Keep files private on a shared PC →
If it's lost or stolen

On Windows Home, the drive may not be encrypted at all, so it can be read in another computer.

Files you lock with Clavis stay locked either way. Check your PC →
In your cloud folder

Anyone who gets into your OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox account can open what you sync.

Encrypt before it syncs. The cloud stores only scrambled data. Encrypt before you sync →

09Before you download

Fair questions

More answers in the Help centre, or write to hello@clavisenc.com.

Should I use the installer or the Microsoft Store version?

Both use the same encryption. The installer from the download page adds the File Explorer right-click menu, the command line and USB stick setup. The Microsoft Store version has none of those three.

Is the original file really gone?

Not always. The overwrite is best effort: on SSDs and USB drives, and where backups or File History exist, earlier copies can survive. For the drive itself, keep device encryption or BitLocker on. See how Shred handles other copies.

Will I always be able to open my files?

Decrypting is part of the free version. It runs on your own PC, offline and with no account, so opening the files you've locked doesn't depend on a Clavis server.
