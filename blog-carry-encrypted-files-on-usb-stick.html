How to carry encrypted files on a USB stick and open them on another PC

A USB stick (a flash drive, or pen drive) goes everywhere: to the office, your accountant at tax time, a college lab, the print shop that prints your forms, your parents' PC. So do the files on it: passport and ID scans, tax papers, payslips, a project report. Encrypting the files is the easy half. The hard half is opening them on a PC that isn't yours, where you can't install software, and where Windows 11 Home can't use BitLocker To Go anyway. Since version 3.14, Clavis Encrypt can put itself on the stick, so the stick carries both the locked files and the program that opens them.

Short answer: to carry encrypted files on a USB stick and open them on other Windows PCs without installing anything there, open USB Stick in the Clavis Encrypt sidebar on your own PC, pick the drive and press Set up stick (Clavis Pro). Clavis copies itself (about 55 MB), your vault and a page called Open my files.html onto the stick. On another PC, double-click Clavis.exe in the stick's Clavis Encrypt folder, unlock with your usual password, and press Finish and remove stick when you're done: nothing is installed or left behind on that PC by Clavis. If the PC won't run programs from USB sticks, double-click Open my files.html instead: it opens your .enc files in the web browser, without internet. Adding only that page to a stick is free.

What you need

  • Clavis Encrypt 3.14 or later on your own Windows PC, from the download page (installer or portable ZIP).
  • A USB stick, memory card, or portable USB SSD or hard drive. Clavis never lists internal disks. If Windows has a connected USB drive switched off, Clavis says so: open Disk Management, right-click the disk and choose Online.
  • Clavis Pro to copy Clavis and your vault onto the stick, and to update it later. Adding the browser page and repairing a stick are free (plans).

Set up the stick (on your own PC)

  1. Plug in the stick, open Clavis and choose USB Stick in the sidebar. Pick your drive.
  2. Check What goes on the stick: Clavis Encrypt, your vault (so your usual password unlocks it), your licence on Pro and Premium, a page that opens your files in a web browser, and an Encrypted files folder.
  3. Press Set up stick. You can press Stop at any time; if you stop or the stick is unplugged, nothing is left half-copied.
  4. When Your USB stick is ready. appears, press Encrypt files onto the stick. It opens the stick's Encrypted files folder; put your files there and encrypt them as usual (right-click → Clavis → Encrypt, or Encrypt & Decrypt in the app).

On Clavis Free, Add the page only puts just Open my files.html and the Encrypted files folder on the stick; each file then opens in the browser with its password. The Microsoft Store version of Clavis can also add the page and the folder, but copying the app itself needs the version from clavisenc.com.

A tip for flash drives: encrypt files on your PC first and then copy the .enc files to the stick. Deleting from flash memory is best effort, so files that sat on the stick unencrypted can leave old copies behind (what deleting really does).

Open your files on another PC

  1. Plug in the stick, open it in File Explorer and double-click Clavis.exe in the Clavis Encrypt folder. A USB stick mode badge shows at the top of the window. Windows SmartScreen may warn the first time; choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Unlock with your usual vault password and open or decrypt your files as you would at home. Clavis keeps its vault, settings and temporary working copies on the stick, not on the PC.
  3. When you're done, press Finish and remove stick (the window's close button asks the same). Clavis locks your vault, removes its working files and closes. Then unplug the stick.

Running from a stick, Clavis adds nothing to the PC: no right-click menu, no activity history, no Windows Hello, and it doesn't download updates there. Secure notes, Sharing and Activity are hidden on the other PC. If a file is still open in another program, such as Word, Clavis asks you to save and close it there first, so it can remove its working copy.

If the PC won't run programs from the stick

Some PCs block programs on USB sticks: office PCs with strict policies, and PCs with Smart App Control turned on, which blocks apps it can't vouch for. That's what the page is for.

  1. Open the stick in File Explorer and double-click Open my files.html. It opens in the web browser and works without internet; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Choose or drop a .enc file, type your password and press Open.
  3. View it, or save a readable copy with Save to Downloads (in Edge and Chrome, Save to… lets you choose where). Delete the readable copy when you're done: it's an ordinary file on that PC.

No stick with you? The same thing works online at clavisenc.com/open.html, on a phone too, and the file is still decrypted on the device, not uploaded. To lock a file on a phone before sending it, see how to encrypt a file on your phone.

Keep the stick up to date and safe

Plug the stick into your own PC and open USB Stick. While that window is open, Clavis compares the program files on the stick with its own copy, so a stick that someone changed doesn't quietly record your password.

What it can and can't do

Three things stay on your own PC on purpose: your key file, Windows Hello and your recovery key. If your vault uses a key file, carry it separately (for example on your phone), and never keep the recovery key on the stick: whoever finds both has everything. And keep a second copy of your files somewhere else, because USB sticks fail without warning.

Questions

How do I open encrypted files on another computer without installing anything?

Carry the program on the stick. Clavis Encrypt's USB Stick window (Clavis Pro) copies Clavis, your vault and your encrypted files onto a USB stick; on another Windows PC you double-click Clavis.exe on the stick and unlock with your password. Nothing is installed there. Where programs from USB sticks are blocked, double-click Open my files.html on the stick to open the files in the web browser, without internet.

How do I put a password on a USB stick without BitLocker on Windows 11 Home?

Encrypt the files on it. With Clavis Encrypt (free) each file becomes a locked .enc file that only your password opens, and with Clavis Pro the USB Stick window also puts Clavis on the stick so the files open on other PCs. BitLocker To Go isn't available on Windows 11 Home.

Is there portable encryption software that runs from a USB stick without admin rights?

Yes. Clavis Encrypt runs from a USB stick on Windows without administrator rights and keeps its data on the stick. VeraCrypt's portable mode, by its own documentation, needs administrator privileges on each PC.

Does it leave any trace on the other PC?

Clavis installs nothing and keeps its vault, settings and working copies on the stick, and Finish and remove stick removes its working files. Windows itself still records that a USB device was connected and which programs ran, programs such as Word may keep recent-files lists, and files you save from the browser page stay until you delete them.

What if the office or print shop PC blocks programs on USB sticks?

Double-click Open my files.html on the stick. It opens your .enc files in Edge, Chrome or Firefox without internet and without uploading anything. If the PC blocks USB storage entirely, neither will work; clavisenc.com/open.html opens files from other places, such as email or a phone.

What happens if I lose the USB stick?

The files and the vault on it stay encrypted, so whoever finds it needs your password. Use a strong password, keep your recovery key and key file somewhere other than the stick, and keep a second copy of your files.

Does my Clavis Pro licence work on other PCs?

Pro and Premium licences are copied to the stick so paid features work on other PCs. A key made for one PC, such as a trial key, makes Clavis run as Free on other PCs; your files still open as usual.

Why can't I copy a 5 GB video to my encrypted USB stick?

Sticks formatted as FAT32 can't hold files of 4 GB or more. Clavis checks before it encrypts and tells you. Use a smaller file, or reformat the stick as exFAT (this erases everything on it).
